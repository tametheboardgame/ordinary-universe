# Visual style guide — working brief v0.1

**Status:** Direction, not yet an approved final art style.

## Desired look

- Original, clean contemporary graphic-novel illustration. Drawn, not a collage or photo-comic.
- Believable, slightly imperfect ordinary adults in everyday British environments.
- Cinematic framing: epic low angles, shallow-looking perspective, deliberate silhouettes, exaggerated backlight and stormy skies where justified.
- Dramatic visual language must contrast with mundane objects (keys, mugs, lanyards, chairs, bins).
- Understated facial acting; never wacky meme faces. Dry humour.
- Muted everyday palette, with occasional warm or cool dramatic accent lighting.
- Text and art must stay readable at phone width.

## Avoid

- Tracing or copying DC character costumes, symbols, actors or poster layouts.
- Genuine magic, energy beams, futuristic armour or extraordinary tech.
- Superhero bodybuilder physiques for ordinary people.
- Inconsistent AI-generated faces between panels.
- Long exposition bubbles, unreadable lettering or final punchlines hidden in tiny captions.

## Lettering and panels

Use a standard comic lettering family with clear rights for web publication. Normal-case dialogue; occasional uppercase epic caption, only for editorial voice. Leave generous speech-bubble safe areas during composition. A common four-panel grid and title system should be established and tested before any pilot is marked 'approved'.

## Visual approval checkpoints

1. 2–3 alternative style frames, using **the same ordinary domestic scene** so the aesthetic can be compared.
2. One agreed style sheet: line weight, colours, shadows, panel margins, text sizing and export settings.
3. Model sheets for Martin, Gary and the seven distinct Justins.
4. OU-001 complete storyboard and test lettering before committing to batch illustration.
