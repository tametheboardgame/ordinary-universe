# Visual style guide — house style decision v0.2

**Status:** **Style A selected and approved as the default Ordinary Universe visual direction (23 October 2026).** Character likenesses, lettering, final palette and model sheets still require separate approval.

## Approved house style: A — Cinematic Graphic-Novel

The approved reference is **column A of the three-way Man of Carbon comparison** shown during creative review. Styles B and C were explored but not selected as the general house style.

Style A uses detailed drawn realism (not photographs), ordinary believable faces, expressive but controlled linework, rich atmospheric environments, dramatic shadows and film-like lighting. It should look like an overly serious prestige superhero adaptation of a normal British person's life.

**The contrast is everything:** the visuals are monumental; the events remain unremarkable.

## Special alternate style

The deliberate exception is **Justin Snyder's Justin League**, a longer, darker, more self-important recut of The Justin League. It may use a hyper-stylised, extremely dramatic treatment of A, with selected touches of textured Style C, but is **not** the default look. See [special treatment bible](../bible/justin-snyders-justin-league.md).

## Desired look

- Original, clean contemporary graphic-novel illustration. Drawn, not a collage or photo-comic.
- Believable, slightly imperfect ordinary adults in everyday British environments.
- Cinematic framing: epic low angles, shallow-looking perspective, deliberate silhouettes, exaggerated backlight and stormy skies where justified.
- Dramatic visual language must contrast with mundane objects (keys, mugs, lanyards, chairs, bins).
- Understated facial acting; never wacky meme faces. Dry humour.
- Muted everyday palette, with occasional warm or cool dramatic accent lighting.
- Text and art must stay readable at phone width.

## Avoid

- Tracing or copying DC character costumes, symbols, actors or poster layouts.
- Genuine magic, energy beams, futuristic armour or extraordinary tech.
- Superhero bodybuilder physiques for ordinary people.
- Inconsistent AI-generated faces between panels.
- Long exposition bubbles, unreadable lettering or final punchlines hidden in tiny captions.

## Colour and composition principles for A

- Start from believable colours and grounded clothing; use warm rim-light versus deep navy/charcoal shadows for contrast when the scene supports it.
- Give ordinary objects conspicuously heroic placement without turning them into sci-fi artefacts.
- Maintain readable faces and dialogue areas; reserve true high contrast for expressive focal points.
- Preserve the drawn-ink quality and anatomically believable character design across every panel.
- Keep the doctor, shed, supermarket and pub recognisably real, even when the camera angle is absurdly epic.

## Lettering and panels

Use a standard comic lettering family with clear rights for web publication. Normal-case dialogue; occasional uppercase epic caption, only for editorial voice. Leave generous speech-bubble safe areas during composition. A common four-panel grid and title system should be established and tested before any pilot is marked 'approved'.

## Visual approval checkpoints

1. **Completed:** Compared three treatments (A cinematic, B clean contemporary, C textured indie) using a common Man of Carbon sequence.
2. **Completed at concept level:** Style A selected as the default direction. Detailed production tokens (colours, line weight, lettering margins and rendering method) still to be standardised.
3. Model sheets for Martin, Gary and the seven distinct Justins.
4. OU-001 complete storyboard and test lettering before committing to batch illustration.

**Approved:** Overall Style A direction only. **Not yet approved:** Finished master style reference asset, precise colour/lettering tokens, and individual character model sheets.
