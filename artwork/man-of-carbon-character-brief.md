# Man of Carbon — Character design brief v0.1

**Status:** Draft design specification, not a locked model sheet.  
**Style:** Ordinary Universe **A: Cinematic Graphic-Novel**.  
**Reference:** [Character profile](../characters/man-of-carbon.md).

## Character identity

Martin Clarke (working civilian name), an ordinary British man in his late 30s to mid-40s. He believes an utterly standard biological fact confers a heroic calling. He is neither stupid nor brave beyond human proportions; he is earnest, intense and convinced that life is quietly asking something monumental of him.

## Distinctive but credible visual identity

- Medium build, neither muscular nor visibly comic-book heroic. Slightly stooped when relaxed, upright when giving himself an inspirational speech.
- Lived-in face with faint under-eye shadows and natural lines, slightly unkempt brown hair, light stubble, dark-framed glasses.
- Default clothes: soft, ordinary red-and-charcoal checked shirt worn open over a grey tee, dark jeans, practical trainers. No heroic chest badge, red cape or similar borrowed iconography.
- Visual recognisability comes from a consistent silhouette, his roundish glasses, scruffy hair and a particular earnest intensity, **not** fantasy props.
- When he 'transforms' into Man of Carbon, he does not change clothing. Only the camera angle, weather and lighting change.

## Reference sheet requirements

1. Clear front, 3/4 and side full-body views on neutral background, same face/proportions/outfit throughout.
2. Expression row: neutral, startled revelation, grave heroic resolve and faint disappointment.
3. Three ordinary props: NHS appointment slip (fictional/unbranded), shopping bag and shed key.
4. Show him at two scales: panel-friendly bust and full-body. Every view should be usable to anchor future artwork.
5. Keep labels outside main figures; no speech bubbles or poster branding within the model sheet.

## Storytelling character beats

- **Normal Martin:** Overthinking his daily commute or an appointment.
- **Self-appointed hero:** Gazes into middle distance as if hearing the call of destiny.
- **Human reveal:** Quietly says something embarrassed or polite when faced with the obvious.
- **Recurring space:** His garden shed, treated as a private stronghold.

## Reject if

- He looks like a celebrity or a costumed DC hero.
- Every expression is caricatured or unserious.
- His clothing or face visibly changes between angles.
- The sheet is a finished comic strip rather than a consistent reference.
- The visual gag requires a cape or powers.

## Approval gates

**Gate A:** Select canonical face, proportions, hairstyle, wardrobe and glasses.  
**Gate B:** Verify consistency in a three-angle turnaround.  
**Gate C:** Approve neutral and dramatic-lit reference frames.  
**Gate D:** Produce OU-001 storyboard and final lettered panels.

Only after Gate A–C approval should the approved sheet be committed as the authoritative visual reference.
