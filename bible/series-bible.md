# The Ordinary Universe — Series Bible v0.1

**Format:** *The Ordinary Verse*, a web-first comic-book anthology containing multiple two-page stories per numbered issue.  
**First home:** DaveHub.  
**Strapline:** *Extraordinary stories. Entirely ordinary people.*  
**Status:** Working creative canon; all designs and scripts still subject to approval.

## 1. Premise

This is a shared superhero-style universe where every apparently legendary hero and villain is an entirely normal person whose title is a literal description of something mundane. Their identities have usually been wildly overinterpreted by themselves. They do not possess superpowers.

The universe uses all the storytelling language of modern superhero epics: grand origin stories, deep shadows, dramatic weather, grim monologues, elaborate continuity, rivalries, post-credit teases and team-ups. The actual plots concern lost keys, work lanyards, recycling bins, meeting rooms, mild disagreements and group chats.

**The essential joke:** Nobody in the world acknowledges that any of this is funny. Every character treats their own experience as deeply consequential.

## 2. Non-negotiable rules

1. **Literal identity:** Names refer to a commonplace fact, occupation, behaviour, everyday possession or actual personal name.
2. **No powers:** No magic, super-strength, genuine alien biology, miraculous technology, impossible feats or actual end-of-world danger.
3. **Sincere people:** Nobody explains the joke, breaks the fourth wall or laughs at their own codename.
4. **Epic framing, tiny stakes:** Film a forgotten bin as an extinction-level event.
5. **Mundane consequences and obsessive continuity:** A booking problem may echo through several future strips.
6. **Recognisably British texture:** Homes, councils, pubs, supermarkets, offices, leisure centres, trains, WhatsApp and the weather.
7. **Character before pun:** Give recurring characters individual habits, motives, contradictions and relationships.
8. **Original designs:** Parody the heroic storytelling grammar, not the exact likenesses, artwork, logos or costumes of existing franchises.
9. **Kindly underneath:** Normal people helping with chairs or checking a door are not idiots. The disproportionate cinematic presentation is the joke.

**Comic engine:** Monumental declaration → understandable human problem → disproportionate heroic response → understated reveal. A silent last panel often beats a spoken punchline.

## 3. Heroes

### Man of Carbon
**Civilian identity:** Martin Clarke (working name).  
**Literal truth:** A carbon-based human, like everybody else.  
**Personality:** Well-meaning, earnest, slightly grandiose, easily drawn to sweeping conclusions.  
**Home:** Ordinary semi-detached house; his garden shed is the Fortress of Solitude.  
**Recurring bit:** Refers to ordinary bodily functions as 'my abilities'.  
**Longer arc:** Learns he is not unique but is unwilling to abandon his calling.  
**Tagline:** *He is one of us. Literally.*

### The Dark Night
**Civilian identity:** Gary Knight.  
**Literal truth:** Regularly awake during the hours of darkness.  
**Personality:** Sleep-deprived, conscientious, anxious about domestic security.  
**Setting:** Gotham, Nottinghamshire; the hallway, kitchen and back door of his house.  
**Recurring bit:** His 'patrol' is checking the same door repeatedly.  
**Tagline:** *When darkness falls, he's usually still up.*

### Wonder Woman
**Civilian identity:** Wendy Palmer (working name).  
**Literal truth:** A woman who wonders about things.  
**Personality:** Curious, observant, persistent; surprisingly grounded.  
**Recurring bit:** Her questions reveal flaws in everyone's supposedly epic reasoning.  
**Tagline:** *She has questions.*

### The Green Lanyard
**Civilian identity:** Graham Finch (working name).  
**Literal truth:** His workplace access badge hangs on a green lanyard.  
**Personality:** Procedural, security-conscious, deeply policy-minded.  
**Recurring bit:** Can open certain doors, unless the permissions have expired.  
**Tagline:** *Access granted. Usually.*

### Aqua Man
**Civilian identity:** Alan Waters (working name).  
**Literal truth:** Drinks water, showers and swims recreationally.  
**Personality:** Serious about hydration and leisure-centre etiquette.  
**Recurring bit:** Treats a swimming timetable as control over the seas.  
**Tagline:** *The ocean is mostly water. So is he.*

### The Flash
**Civilian identity:** Phil Marsh (working name).  
**Literal truth:** Photographer who uses a camera flash.  
**Personality:** Perfectionist about lighting, often misses the actual moment.  
**Recurring bit:** 'Could you do that again?'  
**Tagline:** *Capturing the moment. Sometimes.*

### Cyber Man
**Civilian identity:** Simon Patel (working name).  
**Literal truth:** Works in IT and uses computers.  
**Personality:** Competent, practical, quietly irritated by vague tickets.  
**Recurring bit:** Cosmic technological crises are usually password resets.  
**Tagline:** *Half man. Half technology user.*

### Robin
**Civilian identity:** Robin Chapman.  
**Literal truth:** He is named Robin.  
**Personality:** Pleasant, independent, not interested in a crime-fighting partnership.  
**Recurring bit:** Gary insists they are partners; Robin recalls a single barbecue.  
**Tagline:** *He's Robin.*

## 4. The Justin League

An exclusive organisation of seven perfectly normal men united only by the fact they are all named Justin. They insist this is a destiny. They are not otherwise a superhero team, and the other heroes are not members.

**Headquarters:** The Hall of Justins, a £45 pub function room available on alternate Thursdays, kitchenette included.

**Roster:**
- **Justin from Accounts:** Unappointed organiser, keeps minutes and fears overspending.
- **Hi-Vis Justin:** Practical and helpful; carries tools.
- **Gym Justin:** Trains intensely for missions that require no exertion.
- **Laptop Justin:** Turns every problem into a spreadsheet.
- **Dad Justin:** Has to collect somebody by seven.
- **Quiet Justin:** Rarely speaks, usually makes sense.
- **Other Justin:** Feels the nickname is unfair.

Their group chat is muted by most of them. They cannot agree who was the original Justin or who leads. One email intended for one Justin usually mobilises all seven.

**Motto:** *No powers. Just Justins.*

## 5. Antagonists

Their 'evil' identities describe ordinary inconveniences or opposing but reasonable preferences.

- **The Joker:** Tells repetitive jokes; thinks every punchline deserves a laugh.
- **The Riddler:** Pub quiz host; takes his questions very seriously.
- **Two-Face:** Friendly in meetings, disparaging afterwards.
- **Mr Freeze:** Wants the office thermostat set to 17°C.
- **Poison Ivy:** Ivy once gave party guests food poisoning at a barbecue.
- **Catwoman:** Owns three cats and maintains a meticulous feeding timetable.
- **The Penguin:** Prefers a dinner jacket for practically every occasion.
- **Les Luthor:** Leslie, a property developer, disputes Martin's parking.
- **Doomsday:** Monday morning, framed as a regularly recurring apocalypse.

There are no actual masterminds or monsters. The 'villain' may simply have a different preference.

## 6. Key locations

- **Metropolis:** A nondescript British city with two shopping centres and underwhelming buses.
- **Gotham:** The real village in Nottinghamshire, filmed as a terrifying criminal capital.
- **Fortress of Solitude:** Martin's garden shed, containing half-finished DIY supplies.
- **Hall of Justins:** Pub function room with unreliable availability.
- **Watchtower:** Ordinary office building the Justins are not entitled to enter.
- **Council Leisure Centre:** Aqua Man's 'kingdom', with lane restrictions.
- **The Group Chat:** The great cross-franchise battleground, despite not being a place.

## 7. Escalation and continuity

**Solo:** Introduce name and behaviour.  
**Encounter:** Two characters interpret the same normal situation differently.  
**Team-up:** Scheduling or admin issues bring people together.  
**Event:** One accidental message or booking problem ripples through several casts.

Track mundane props and their consequences. Reward long-term readers without making new strips inaccessible.

## 8. Format and visual identity

**The standard comic format is one composed double-page spread of 8–12 panels, targeting 10 panels by default.** This is an approved replacement for the original four-panel concept. The artist can vary panel shapes and sizes for dramatic pacing rather than use a fixed grid. Compose left and right pages as one coherent landscape spread, protecting the central gutter and establishing an unambiguous left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading sequence. A large establishing panel, silent reaction panels and a dry closing beat are encouraged. Use Style A cinematic graphic-novel drawing, believable people, everyday clothes, dramatic framing and chiaroscuro. Avoid photo-comics, copied costumes, broad caricatures and dense lettering.

**For DaveHub**, preserve the double-spread composition as the desktop master while providing individually exportable panels in the same reading order for mobile scrolling or a guided panel reader. Original artwork must not be shrunk to illegibility on a phone. Storytelling rules, layout principles and export requirements: [Double-spread layout guide](../artwork/double-spread-layout.md).

A strip needs a clear visual joke, readable phone-size dialogue, consistent character designs and an accessible transcript. Keep master art separate from web exports.

**Publishing identity:** **The Ordinary Universe** names the shared fictional universe; **The Ordinary Verse** names the anthology comic. A numbered issue contains several 2-page stories in a fixed reading order. For example, Issue #1 opens with *The Origin of Man of Carbon* on pages 1–2. The issue is the main reading unit, not a collection of unrelated web posts.

**DaveHub:** A dedicated *The Ordinary Verse* web-comic section with cover, issue contents, start/continue reading, archive and story navigation. Desktop preserves the authored double spreads; mobile reads individually exported panels in order. See the publishing specification.

### Approved visual direction (23 October 2026)

**Style A — Cinematic Graphic-Novel** is the general house style. In the reviewed three-way sample, A was the dramatic, atmospheric and realistically illustrated option. The look is approved at the level of overall direction only: individual character faces, precise lettering, and the final colour spec are pending.

### The deliberately different edition

**Justin Snyder's Justin League** is a special, overblown 'director's cut' of the original **The Justin League** narrative. It contains extra gravitas, extra panels, selected changed panel order and essentially the same ending. For this *exception*, use hyper-stylised A with optional textured Style C influences. Full concept: [Justin Snyder's Justin League](justin-snyders-justin-league.md).

## 10. Release criteria

A strip must work for someone unfamiliar with DC; reward those who are familiar; have a clear human story and visual payoff; avoid real powers; fit phone reading; retain visual continuity; include an HTML-readable transcript; and avoid copying third-party designs.

**First production milestone:** Approve the illustration style, Martin and Gary model sheets, at least three distinct Justins, and a reusable 8–12-panel double-spread layout/lettering master. Then produce OU-001, OU-002 and OU-006. These are scripts, not finished published comics.
